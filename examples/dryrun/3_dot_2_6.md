# Dry run 3: dot 2 to 6, the units linked into one accelerator

Every number on this page is what the tools printed when it was written; no test checks them (D98), so they can drift as the code moves.

dot 1 (`2_dot_1.md`) is two accelerators that pass the N products through L1. dot 2 to 6 keep the products inside one accelerator: one group of two units joined by a link, so one streamer set, one START and one control unit (D148, D157). Each is a few editor steps from dot 1's step 8. This page builds the five, runs them, and sets the model's estimate against SYN2's synthesis and simulation of the same designs, the check DRY1 left for after the dry runs.

How to read it:

- **Cycles are exact.** The Estimate's cycles equal SYN2's simulated cycles at all five points, and each SNAX-GVSoC COUNTER equals its expected COUNTER, the cycles plus a fill of 3. The window is 944 for dot 2 to 4 and 953 for dot 5 and 6, 274 and 265 under dot 1's 1218: the link runs both units at once and drops the core's handover between two accelerators.
- **Area is within 0.6 %** on both technologies, the multiplier or the Mac nearly all of it. The largest gap, dot 4's +0.6 % on nangate45, is its adder tree: the Estimate prices the block as CHAR1 measured it alone (2 712 µm²), with its `first` input, while dot 4's tree runs one job and firtool folds `first` in (2 342 µm²; dot 2's and dot 3's trees keep it and match to the µm²). C§20 does not name this.
- **Energy is 1.01 to 1.08 high**, and the parts say where. The multiplier and the Mac are 1.03 to 1.04 and the control unit 1.00 to 1.04 everywhere, as in CHAR1's checks. dot 4's and dot 5's sums come out at 0.17 to 0.46 of SYN2's, C§20's named limit: a sum fed by the Mac fires once (the tree) or 8 times (the serial sum) but sees the Mac's outputs change 256 times; dot 6's serial sum, behind a buffer that loads once, is within 1 %. The sums are small, so dot 4 and dot 5 stay within 3.1 %.
- **The buffer links.** dot 2's `tmp0` buffer is the gap, 1.59× on nangate45 and 1.49× on nangate15, not among C§20's limits, which name deep buffers 1.4 to 1.8× high on nangate45 only; CHAR1 checked no design with a buffer link. It does load at every beat, 256 times in 258 cycles, and still costs 6.07 pJ a cycle in the design against the 9.49 pJ CHAR1 measured alone in its stream window (`link/d1_w8`); this page does not explain that (open item 112). dot 6's `out_partial` matches: the model counts a buffer's loads as its producer's outputs, its firings over its T, and the Mac gives its partials once per job, so the buffer loads once, not 256 times, and costs 0.979 nJ on nangate45 (1.03 of SYN2's 0.950) and 0.260 nJ on nangate15 (0.99), and dot 6's kernel is 1.039 and 1.028 of SYN2's.
- **The L1 placeholder is apart.** It is 18.008 nJ (nangate45) for each of dot 2 to 6 and 36.011 nJ for dot 1, more than the kernel itself; SYN2 has no memory, so it is not in any ratio. It is what makes the linked designs cheaper than dot 1 in the editor's total (42.5 to 45.3 nJ against 61.1 nJ); their kernels alone are 24.5 to 27.3 nJ against dot 1's 25.1.

So on dot 4 the model holds to about 1 % in energy and area from W = 4 on, on both technologies; its two errors are fixed costs that a large W hides.

```systemverilog
    .io_in_0 (_blocks_0_4_io_out_0),
    ...
    .io_in_7 (_blocks_0_4_io_out_7),
    .io_out  (io_units_1_out),
    .io_fire (io_units_1_fire)
  );
```

The tree has no `first` here: it runs one job of one beat, so firtool folds it in (section 2's area note). **The control.** One unit drives both, from `snax_forge_dot_4_dry_dot_ctrl.sv`:

```systemverilog
  reg         busy;
  reg  [31:0] acc_2_jobs;
  reg  [31:0] acc_1_job_beats;
  reg  [31:0] acc_1_job_beats_counter;
  reg  [31:0] acc_1_jobs_counter;
  reg         acc_1_v_0;
  reg  [31:0] acc_2_jobs_counter;
  reg         acc_2_v_0;
  wire        _GEN =
    busy & acc_1_jobs_counter != acc_2_jobs & io_in_0_valid & io_in_1_valid;
  wire        acc_2_gives = busy & acc_2_jobs_counter != acc_2_jobs & acc_1_v_0;
  wire        acc_2_frozen = acc_2_v_0 & ~io_out_ready;
  wire        acc_2_fire = acc_2_gives & ~acc_2_frozen;
  wire        acc_1_frozen = acc_1_v_0 & ~acc_2_fire;
  wire        acc_1_fire = _GEN & ~acc_1_frozen;
  wire        done = busy & acc_2_v_0 & ~acc_2_frozen & acc_2_jobs_counter == acc_2_jobs;
  ...
      acc_1_gives = _GEN & acc_1_job_beats_counter == acc_1_job_beats - 32'h1;
  ...
  assign io_units_1_fire = acc_2_fire;
  assign io_units_0_fire = acc_1_fire;
  assign io_units_0_first = acc_1_job_beats_counter == 32'h0;
```

The Mac fires on every beat both inputs are valid and counts its 256 beats in `acc_1_job_beats_counter`; on the last it gives (`acc_1_gives`), and its result sits in `acc_1_v_0` for its latency of 1, where the tree takes it (`acc_2_gives` on `acc_1_v_0`) and holds its own in `acc_2_v_0` until `out` is ready. `acc_1_frozen` is the link: the Mac stalls only while its result waits and the tree does not fire, the wire's backpressure. `io_units_0_first` restarts the Mac's sums at a job's first beat. The registers are the estimate's 164 control bits: `busy` (1), `acc_2_jobs`, `acc_1_job_beats`, `acc_1_job_beats_counter`, `acc_1_jobs_counter` and `acc_2_jobs_counter` (32 each), `acc_1_v_0`, `acc_2_v_0` and the done pulse `io_done_REG` (1 each). `acc_1_jobs_counter` is open item 103's: a Mac on a wire into a one-job consumer could borrow its test.
